From abelian square-free words over four or more letters to aa2f words over three letters Computational experiments, a lifting principle, a sharp limit for four-letter decorations, and fragile near misses in g85

V. KeränenWorking notes, 25 October 2026Experiments run with Claude Code

The question

Can abelian square-free (a2f) words over four letters, such as the fixed point of g85, be turned systematically into very long, ideally infinite, aa2f or aa2fr words over {a, b, c}? Letters may be added, either inserted between existing letters or used to replace some occurrences of a letter. Both operations keep the a2f property.

Recall that a ternary word is aa2f if its only abelian squares have period 1. It is aa2fr if, in addition, it contains no factor yxxz with y ≠ x ≠ z. Since xxxx is an abelian square of period 2, the maximal runs of an aa2fr word have length 1 or 3.

1 · The molecule alphabet and a lifting principle

Write a ternary word t as a sequence of maximal runs. Each run xk becomes one letter, a molecule, of the alphabet

Γ6 = {a, b, c, aaa, bbb, ccc} (aa2fr),   Γ9 = Γ6 ∪ {aa, bb, cc} (aa2f).

Denote the resulting molecule word by μ(t). Neighbouring molecules always have different base letters. This generalises the double-width letters ȧ, ḃ, ċ of the geometric note, with one important difference: here a molecule of width k counts k times in the ternary Parikh vector.

Proposition 1 (lifting). If t is aa2fr, then μ(t) is an a2f word over the six-letter alphabet Γ6. If t is aa2f, then μ(t) is a2f over Γ9.

Proof. Suppose UV is a factor of μ(t) with Ψ(U) = Ψ(V) over the molecule alphabet. Expanding the molecules gives a factor uv of t with the same ternary Parikh vector and the same length, so uv is an abelian square of period |u|. If |u| ≥ 2, this contradicts the aa2f property. If |u| = 1, then U = V is one molecule of width 1, and two equal molecules would be adjacent. That is impossible because the runs are maximal. ∎

Verified on the published words. aa2fr3268 has 1528 molecules over Γ6: 213 a, 225 b, 220 c, 296 aaa, 298 bbb, 276 ccc. aa2f25379 has 12587 molecules over Γ9. Both molecule words are a2f.

The converse fails, and the reason explains everything that follows. A real abelian square in t can show up in μ(t) in two ways:

  1. Aligned, but weighted. U and V have the same ternary Parikh vector with aaa counted as 3 · a. This is a square modulo the weight map ω: Γ6 → ℤ3, whose kernel has rank 3.
  2. Unaligned. The square starts, ends or is split inside a run of length 2 or 3, so it cuts a molecule.

The second kind is what separates the claim in the geometric note from real aa2f-ness. That claim is correct, because it concerns squares modulo Φ′, which are aligned and count a double-width letter once. But take the actual ternary word w′ obtained from g85(a) by replacing each d with its left neighbour. Its first abelian square of period ≥ 2 already ends at position 14:

w  = abcacdcbcdcadcdbdaba…
w′ = abcacccbccca|accbbaba…

The square ca|ac cuts the double-width letter ȧ = aa. Geometrically, c ȧ c is not a square, but as a ternary word it is.

The obstruction is local. Every rigid construction dies within the first 10 to 20 letters of g85(a), whatever the block length. This fits the known structure of g85: its fixed point contains all a2f factors of length 4 except eight. A fixed coding therefore has to survive almost every local pattern, including cacdc, cbcdc and dcdbd.

For morphisms φ from four to three letters there is also a global obstruction, which would remain even if every local problem were solved. It has three steps.

Kernel. Let M be the 3 × 4 Parikh matrix of φ, whose column for letter x is Ψ(φ(x)). Then Ψ(φ(u)) = M Ψ(u) for every word u. Since M has rank at most 3, it has a nonzero integer kernel vector k, with Mk = 0. Write ℤk = {tk : t ∈ ℤ} for its integer multiples. If xy is a factor with Ψ(x) − Ψ(y) = tk and t ≠ 0, then φ(x) and φ(y) have equal Parikh vectors, so φ(x)φ(y) is an abelian square. Here x and y may have different lengths. For example, with φ(a) = a, φ(b) = b, φ(c) = c and φ(d) = abc, we get k = (1, 1, 1, −1). The factor abcd, which occurs in the g85 fixed point, then maps to abc|abc. Abelian square-freeness of the four-letter word only guarantees Ψ(x) ≠ Ψ(y). It says nothing about differences along k.

Imbalance. Write Ψ(x) = (n/4)(1,1,1,1) + e(x) for a factor x of length n. The error e(x) measures how far the factor is from the ideal letter frequencies 1/4. The Parikh matrix of g85 is circulant, with first column (19, 21, 27, 18), and its eigenvalues are 85 (eigenvector (1,1,1,1)), −8 ± 3i (modulus 8.544) and 7. One application of g85 multiplies lengths by 85 but errors by at most 8.544. The error therefore grows like nα, with α = ln 8.544 / ln 85 ≈ 0.48. Measured on factors of length 10, 10², …, 10⁵, the maximal |e(x)| is 3.3, 11, 32, 96 and 265: about three times larger per factor 10, as 100.48 ≈ 3 predicts. Geometrically, the Parikh vectors of all factors lie in a tube around the diagonal ℝ(1,1,1,1) whose radius at length n is about C n0.48.

Reaching the kernel. Write k = (Σki/4)(1,1,1,1) + k⊥. Here k⊥ ≠ 0, because M(1,1,1,1) is the sum of the columns of M, a nonzero vector with nonnegative entries. A hit Ψ(x) − Ψ(y) = tk needs e(x) − e(y) = tk⊥, so the imbalance must reach at least |k⊥|. If the tube had bounded width W, any φ with |k⊥| > 2W would avoid such aligned squares forever. This happens when all eigenvalues other than the largest have modulus below 1. For g85 the tube keeps widening, so this protection is absent. Differences of size |k⊥| occur once factors are roughly |k⊥|2 long. This is a heuristic, not a theorem: a wide enough tube is necessary for a hit, but some adjacent pair must also land exactly on a point tk. In the experiments above, the morphisms failed locally long before this scale.

3 · A sharp limit for four-letter decorations

The experiments in section 2 fix the skeleton in advance. The decisive test leaves the skeleton free. Fix a partition of Γ6 into k classes, and search over all molecule words whose ternary image is aa2fr and whose shadow is a2f over k letters. The shadow is the word obtained by replacing each molecule with the name of its class. These ternary words are exactly the decorated k-letter a2f words in the sense of the question, where a letter is split into the molecules of its class.

Proposition 2 (computer-verified, exhaustive). Let t be a ternary word all of whose maximal runs have length 1 or 3, and suppose t is aa2f. If, for some partition of Γ6 into four classes, the shadow of μ(t) is abelian square-free, then |t| ≤ 85.

The bound is attained. All 65 partitions were searched to completion, the largest tree having 3.6 · 10⁶ nodes. The maximum 85 occurs, for example, for the classes {a,b}, {c,ccc}, {aaa}, {bbb}:

w = aaacaaabbbcccaaabaaacaaabbbcbbbabbbcccaaabcabbbcccaaabaaacaaabbbcbbbabbbcccaaabbbcbbb

The extremal word and its shadow

The 85-letter word w above has a symmetric structure. Let

w5a = aaacaa,   w5b = bbcbbb,   w18 = abbbcccaaabaaacaaa,
w36 = w18 · mir(w18),

where mir(u) is the reversal of u with a and b exchanged. Then

w = w5a · w36 · c · w36 · w5b.

The shadow is formed by splitting w into its 37 molecules, aaa · c · aaa · bbb · ccc · aaa · b · …, and replacing each by the name of its class: {a,b} → a, {c,ccc} → b, {aaa} → c, {bbb} → d. The class names are arbitrary; any renaming gives an equivalent shadow. The result is the a2f word

ws = cbcdbcacbcdbdadbcabadbcacbcdbdadbcdbd

An exhaustive search shows that ws is an a2f dead end on both sides:

  • to the right, only wsa, wsab and wsaba are a2f;
  • to the left, only aws, baws and abaws are a2f;
  • the longest two-sided a2f extension is aba · ws · aba (43 letters), and it cannot be extended in either direction.

Hence ws is not a factor of any infinite a2f word over four letters, and in particular not of any fixed point of an a2f endomorphism. This matches Proposition 2. The maximum 85 is not just where the search happened to stop: the extremal decorated word has a shadow that is boxed in on both sides. The symmetric extensions (aba on both sides) reflect the symmetric structure of w. That symmetry may be what allows the word to reach 85 letters before both ends get stuck at once.

The decomposition of w and the observation that ws occurs in no known a2f g(a) are due to V. Keränen (22 October 2026). The extension search is computer-verified.

The published words agree with this from the other direction. For aa2fr3268, merging the six molecule letters into 4 or 5 classes creates an abelian square within the first 22 or 51 molecules, respectively. For aa2f25379, merging its nine molecule letters into 4 or 5 classes does so within 15 or 34 molecules. None of the record words is a decorated four- or five-letter a2f word.

with letter counts a 39, b 54, c 54, d 59, e 57. Here b stands for both single b's and single c's. Extending it on both sides gives three quite different answers, depending on which property must be kept.

  1. The shadow alone extends freely. As a five-letter a2f word it extends by 20 000 letters on each side in under half a second, with about 2.6 search nodes per added letter. The resulting two-sided word of 40 263 letters was checked from scratch.
  2. The decorated word cannot be extended at all. Requiring the shadow to stay a2f and the ternary word to stay aa2fr, no molecule can be added on either side. The search is exhaustive, and a brute-force check of all twelve cases confirms it. On the right, appending the single molecule c is blocked only by the shadow square cb|cb. Every other case already fails in the ternary word or by merging runs, for example aba|aab, ba|ba and cabac|ccaba.
  3. Even the ternary word alone is almost stuck. Ignoring the shadow, no letter can be added on the left, even as a plain aa2f word. On the right exactly one letter fits, c, after which the word is maximal both as aa2f and as aa2fr. Both searches are exhaustive.

So the five-letter shadow has plenty of room, and it is the ternary aa2fr word that is boxed in. This is the opposite of the four-letter extremal word above, whose shadow ws itself was the dead end. It resembles the dead-end phenomenon of aa2fr2107, where a long prefix already forces the end of the word. Longer decorated five-letter words therefore require backtracking deep into the word, not just extending its ends. This is consistent with the chronological search stalling near 600 letters.

For aa2f with nine molecules, the four-class question is still open. The best partition, {a,bb,cc}, {b,bbb}, {c,ccc}, {aa,aaa}, reached 303 ternary letters within 3 · 10⁹ nodes without being exhausted.

Consequence. In the aa2fr case, decorating a four-letter a2f word, by splitting letters and partially replacing them by others, can never give words longer than 85, whatever the four-letter word. Inserting one new letter e moves the problem to five letters, and five is exactly the critical case: decorated five-letter words reach at least 603, while a fixed five-letter skeleton such as Pleasants' fails at 24.

4 · Morphisms on the molecule alphabet

Following the design of g85, I looked for morphisms h on Γ6 that commute with the cyclic shift τ: a → b → c → a. Then only h(a) and h(aaa) need to be chosen, and the candidate word is the fixed point hω(a). The images were filled in lazily while the fixed point was being generated.

  • Image lengths ≤ 10 molecules: all 81 length pairs exhausted, longest ternary prefix 154.
  • Image lengths 11–16: at most 218 within 20 s per pair (15 of 36 pairs exhausted).
  • No symmetry (six independent images), lengths ≤ 7: at most 163.

Short molecule morphisms are therefore not enough. This agrees with Rao and Rosenfeld's finding that abelian squares of periods 2–5 are hard to avoid in ternary morphic words.

5 · The five-letter backbone experiment

Section 3 singles out five letters, with backbone letters P, Q, R, S, T standing for the classes {a}, {b,c}, {aaa}, {bbb}, {ccc}. This experiment searches two things at the same time:

  • a morphism H on {P, Q, R, S, T}, with H(x0) beginning with x0, so that the candidate word is the fixed point Hω(x0);
  • a sliding-block rule choosing b or c at every Q. The rule depends either on the 1 or 2 backbone letters to the left (left:1, left:2), or on the parent letter and the offset inside its image (slot). The slot rule turns the whole construction into a coding of a morphism on six letters.

The images and the rule entries are unknowns. They are fixed lazily while the fixed point is being generated, and the search backtracks over them. Every run checks that the decorated ternary word is aa2fr. Runs come in two modes, which differ in one extra test. In a2f mode, the backbone word over P, Q, R, S, T must itself be abelian square-free, which is the decoration idea of section 3. In free mode, the backbone is not checked at all. Besides the length reached, I recorded the forced streak: the longest stretch of molecules that followed entirely from images and rule entries fixed earlier, with no new free decision. A morphism that really works would show a forced streak growing without bound.

There were 42 configurations: image lengths 8–40, three rule types, two modes, each with 6 random option orders × 4 start letters at 15 s per run. Uniform lengths 3–16 were also scanned for all five rule types, left:0 to left:3 and slot. With a single global choice (left:0), every case is exhausted at 62 letters or fewer. The best prefixes were rebuilt independently by iterating H and applying the rule, and the aa2f prefix lengths agree with the search.

Outcome. No self-sustaining backbone was found. The forced streak never exceeds about one or two image blocks (≤ 64 molecules), so every surviving prefix still depends on fresh free decisions. The best fixed-point prefix, 417 letters, stays below the 603 that free five-class search reaches. The choice of b/c rule matters little, which points to the morphism H as the bottleneck. With longer images the a2f requirement on the backbone helps rather than hurts, because it prunes the search.

This does not rule out five-letter backbones. It shows that image lengths up to 40 are not enough when H is found by naive lazy search. The record words have a long-range structure, as the dead-end phenomenon around position 1557 of aa2fr2107 shows. A morphism capturing that structure would plausibly need images in the hundreds, as g85 needed length 85 for four letters.

6 · Fragile near misses inside g85n(a)

Being abelian square-free does not stop a word from coming very close to an abelian square. Two kinds of near miss are considered here:

  • a uxv pattern: a factor uxv with |u| = |v|, Ψ(u) = Ψ(v) and x a single letter. Removing x would leave an abelian square.
  • a uv pattern: a factor uv with |u| = |v| where u and v differ by one letter, i.e. ‖Ψ(u) − Ψ(v)‖1 = 2. Here ‖z‖1 = |za| + |zb| + |zc| + |zd| is the 1-norm (sum of the absolute values of the components) of a vector z ∈ ℤ4; for |u| = |v| the norm ‖Ψ(u) − Ψ(v)‖1 is always even, and it is twice the number of letters of u that would have to be changed to make u and v abelian equivalent.

A lifting rule

g85 is uniform: every image has length 85. Let A be its Parikh matrix, which is invertible. Every uxv pattern of g85(w) with |u| ≥ 85 cuts the block structure in the same way. It comes from a factor p U y V q of w together with a junction inside g85(p) g85(y) g85(q):

u = sufα(g85(p)) · g85(U) · preβ(g85(y)),   x = g85(y)[β+1],   v = g85(y)[β+2 .. 85] · g85(V) · preγ(g85(q)).

Here preβ(z) and sufα(z) are the prefix of length β and the suffix of length α of a word z, and z[i] is its i-th letter, counting from 1. So x is the letter that follows the prefix preβ(g85(y)), and v begins right after x; the ranges are 1 ≤ α ≤ 85 and 0 ≤ β, γ ≤ 84. The condition Ψ(u) = Ψ(v) then becomes

A · (Ψ(V) − Ψ(U)) = L(p, y, q; α, β, γ),

where L is the Parikh difference of the three partial blocks. It depends only on the junction. So a uxv pattern one level up needs a factor p U y V q below whose imbalance δ = Ψ(V) − Ψ(U) equals A−1L. That factor is an exact uxv (δ = 0) or a near miss (δ ≠ 0, small). Over all 64 triples there are 2472 junctions with an integral δ, taking 49 distinct values.

The hierarchy behind 771. Inside g85(a) there is the small uxv pattern

a · bacadcbc · d · cacdbcba · c    (|U| = |V| = 8, Ψ(U) = Ψ(V) = (2, 2, 3, 1))

with flank triple (a, d, c). That triple has exactly three exact junctions, with local half-lengths 87, 89 and 91. Hence 85 · 8 + {87, 89, 91} = 767, 769, 771, which are exactly the three longest uxv patterns of g85²(a). Among triples of three distinct letters, only (a,d,c), (b,a,d), (c,b,a) and (d,c,b) admit exact junctions, and these are cyclic shifts of each other under σ.

Result. Lifting g85²(a) gives the complete list of uxv patterns with |u| ≥ 85 in g85³(a), which has 614 125 letters. There are 3393 of them, all with |u| ≤ 999. The longest is again 771, and every one of them comes from a copy or σ-rotation of the |U| = 8 pattern. None comes from the 771-pattern itself or from a near miss. So the record does not grow by a factor of about 85 per level: the 771-pattern forms once, and its context (c, a, b) cannot be balanced at the next level.

Checks. On g85²(a) the rule reproduces the brute-force list of all 39 uxv patterns with |u| ≥ 85 exactly, with nothing missing or extra. In g85³(a), every predicted pattern was confirmed directly. A brute-force search over the first 150 000 letters, for 85 ≤ |u| ≤ 2500, found exactly the 831 predicted patterns. Showing that 771 stays the record at every level would also require lifting the near misses with δ ≠ 0. That is a finite computation, but it has not been done yet.

7 · Suggested directions

  1. Work on Γ6 (or Γ9) directly. By Proposition 1 the aa2fr problem is the same as finding an infinite a2f word over six letters, with two extra conditions. The first is avoiding squares modulo the weight map ω, which is a Pirillo–Varricchio square modulo a morphism. The second is avoiding unaligned squares, where at most three molecules are cut and each cut changes the counts by a bounded vector. Both conditions are linear with bounded corrections, which is exactly the setting where Rao and Rosenfeld's template method can decide avoidance for a given morphic word. A candidate molecule morphism h could then be proved correct, not just tested.
  2. Five-letter backbones, from data rather than blind search. Section 5 shows that naive lazy search for H stalls at 417. A better route is to grow long decorated five-class words first, beyond 603, with the fast backtracker. Then look for a recognisable morphic structure in their backbones, for example by desubstitution, recurring blocks, or return words, and only after that fix H.
  3. Use the record words as data. Run the joint search with the C++/CUDA backtracker. That needs one extra suffix test on the shadow. It would show whether decorated five-letter words keep growing like plain aa2fr words. On the same 3 · 10⁹-node budget, plain chronological search reached ≥ 1401, and the five-letter variant stalled near 600.
  4. Decide the aa2f four-letter case. With nine molecules, a bound analogous to Proposition 2 may hold. Exhaustive runs for the top partitions are feasible with the C++ engine.
