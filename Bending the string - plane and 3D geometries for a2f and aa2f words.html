Bending the string Plane and 3D geometries for abelian square-free words over four letters and aa2f words over three

V. KeränenWorking notes, 1 November 2026Drawings and checks with Claude Code

The idea

A word is usually written as a straight string of letters. Here the string is bent so that every letter is tied to its own line or curve, and the question is which bending makes abelian squares visible. Several bendings are possible, and they are not equally useful.

  • Letters on their own lines or curves (edges of a square prism, rays, circles). Every occurrence of a sits on the a-line, and the k-th occurrence sits at distance k. This is literally the picture asked for, and abelian squares become "equal advances on every line". But it only redraws the Parikh vector, coordinate by coordinate.
  • Letters as directions. Every occurrence of a letter is a step in one fixed direction, so all a-steps are parallel. The word becomes a lattice curve, and an abelian square becomes a midpoint: a single, simple geometric event. For g85 this curve is self-similar, because g85 acts on it as multiplication by the Gaussian integer −8 + 3i.

The second bending is the one that transforms the setup, and most of the page is about it.

1 · Five ways to bend g85(a)

The prism makes the symmetry σ of g85 visible. Because the edges are in the order a, b, c, d around the axis, a quarter turn of the prism moves every a onto the b-edge, every b onto the c-edge, and so on: it is σ. In g85(a) the factor at positions 21–46 is cabadbabcbdbcbacbcdcacbabd and the suffix at positions 60–85 is dbcbacbcdcacdcbdcdadbdcbca = σ(cabadbabcbdbcbacbcdcacbabd), so the suffix is the factor turned by a quarter turn and moved 39 places along the axis. Such σ-copies are what the circulant Parikh matrix of g85 records, and in the plane picture of (D) and §3 the same quarter turn becomes multiplication by i.

In (A)–(C), a factor uv with |u| = |v| is an abelian square when, on every line or curve, the string advances by the same number of occurrences during u as during v. This is a correct picture, but a factor has to be checked line by line, four separate counts. The geometry of the lines plays no role: replacing the rays of (B) by the circles of (C), or by any other curves, changes nothing, because only the occurrence counts matter.

In (D) and (E) the four counts are combined into one point, and the test becomes geometric. (D) keeps only a shadow of the counts, the plane coordinate z; (E) keeps all of them. (E) is a faithful picture of the walk in ℤ4 of the next section, drawn in three dimensions; see the remark after Proposition 1.

2 · Abelian squares are midpoints

Let the word w = w1w2⋯ be a walk in ℤ4 that starts at P0 = 0 and takes the unit step ex for the letter x, so Pi = Ψ(w1⋯wi). The factor wi+1⋯wi+2p is an abelian square exactly when Pi+p − Pi = Pi+2p − Pi+p, that is, when Pi+p is the midpoint of Pi and Pi+2p.

Proposition 1. A word over {a, b, c, d} is abelian square-free if and only if no point of its walk is the midpoint of two others. Equivalently, the set {P0, P1, …} contains no three-term arithmetic progression.

Proof. The coordinate sum of Pi is i. If Pj = (Pi + Pk)/2, taking coordinate sums gives j = (i + k)/2, so the three points are equally spaced in time and the factor between Pi and Pk is an abelian square. The converse is the computation above. ∎

This is the progression-free viewpoint of Dekking (1978). For ternary words, the same argument gives: a word over {a, b, c} is aa2f if and only if its walk in ℤ3 has no midpoint triple Pi, Pi+p, Pi+2p with p ≥ 2. The allowed triples with p = 1 are exactly the two equal steps of a run xx, so they are straight pieces of the walk.

The tetrahedral walk (E). The four directions ta, tb, tc, td from the centre of a regular tetrahedron to its vertices add up to zero, and any three of them are linearly independent. So the only linear relation among them is ta + tb + tc + td = 0. The walk with step tx for the letter x is the walk of Proposition 1 seen along the diagonal (1, 1, 1, 1), the direction that only records length. Two prefixes of the same length therefore reach the same point exactly when they have the same Parikh vector. Hence a factor wi+1⋯wi+2p is an abelian square if and only if, in the tetrahedral walk, the point at time i + p is the midpoint of the points at times i and i + 2p, with no extra coordinate needed. Unlike the plane walk of (D), it needs no height: in g85²(a) there are 6739 planar midpoint triples with p ≤ 300, but the tetrahedral walk has none with p ≤ 500.

The tetrahedral walk is not new here. In 2004, E. Jensen and V. Keränen drew 4-letter words as walks with tetrahedral steps, including the paths of g85(a), g85²(a) and g98(a), as rotatable 3D graphics (4 Letter Walks in Diamond Lattice, 25 February 2004; local copy 4LetterWalksInDiamondLattice.nb.html). That page credits the vectorgram concept (2003) to Jensen and the tetrahedron concept (2004) to Keränen. Panel (E) redraws that picture; what this page adds is the exact midpoint criterion above and the comparison with the plane walk of (D).

3 · The plane hidden in ℤ4: g85 is a rotation and a dilation

The walk lives in four dimensions, but the cyclic symmetry σ: a → b → c → d → a of g85 suggests the right coordinates. Give the letters the complex directions

a ↦ 1,   b ↦ i,   c ↦ −1,   d ↦ −i,

and let zk be the sum of the directions of w1⋯wk. This is drawing (D): a curve in the Gaussian integers ℤ[i]. Two more coordinates complete the picture, the time t = k and the height hk = #a − #b + #c − #d. The map Ψ ↦ (t, z, h) is the discrete Fourier transform over ℤ/4, so it is injective, and Proposition 1 reads:

w is a2f ⇔ there are no k, p with zk+p = (zk + zk+2p)/2 and hk+p = (hk + hk+2p)/2.

Proposition 2 (g85 is a similarity of the plane). Let w′ = g85(w). At every block boundary,

z′85k = (−8 + 3i) · zk,    h′85k = 7 · hk,    t′ = 85 t.

Proof. g85(σx) = σ(g85(x)), and σ multiplies every direction by i. Hence the displacement of g85(x) is ix times the displacement of g85(a), which is 19 + 21i − 27 − 18i = −8 + 3i (the letter counts of g85(a) are 19, 21, 27, 18). Summing over the letters of w gives the formula for z; the same argument with the signs ±1 gives 19 − 21 + 27 − 18 = 7 for h. ∎

So applying g85 rotates the curve by arg(−8 + 3i) ≈ 159.44° and stretches it by |−8 + 3i| = √73 ≈ 8.544, while the 85 letters of each block fill in the gaps. These are the eigenvalues found earlier in the Parikh-matrix analysis; here they appear as plain geometry. Both identities were checked exactly for g85²(a) and g85³(a).

The height matters. In g85²(a) there are 6739 triples with gap p ≤ 300 whose middle point is the midpoint of the outer two in the plane. None of them is an abelian square, because in every one of them the heights fail to match. The plane carries most of the structure, and the height h is the thin extra coordinate that finally separates the points. The tetrahedral walk of (E) carries both at once: its three coordinates are linear combinations of z and h, and g85 acts on it as a linear map with the eigenvalues −8 ± 3i (the turning plane) and 7 (the axis of h).

5 · Three letters: the Eisenstein walk

For three letters, the directions are the cube roots of unity:

a ↦ 1,   b ↦ ω,   c ↦ ω²,    ω = e2πi/3.

The walk now lives on the triangular lattice of Eisenstein integers ℤ[ω], and no height coordinate is needed: the Fourier transform over ℤ/3 has only the time and this one complex coordinate. The whole Parikh vector of a ternary word is therefore a point of the plane together with the time, and the aa2f condition is purely planar.

Proposition 3. A ternary word is aa2f if and only if its Eisenstein walk has no triple zk, zk+p, zk+2p with p ≥ 2 and zk+p the midpoint of the other two. It is aa2fr if, in addition, its straight pieces have length 1 or 3.

Checked on aa2fr3268: no midpoint triple with p ≥ 2, and exactly 1740 with p = 1, two for each of its 870 runs of length 3. A ternary morphism that commutes with the cyclic shift a → b → c → a, like the molecule morphisms tried in the experiments note, acts on this plane as multiplication by a single Eisenstein integer, just as g85 acts by −8 + 3i.

6 · Why straight lines, and what curves would do

Abelian equivalence forgets the order of letters, so the geometry must forget it too: the steps must commute. Translations of the plane commute, and that is why straight directions work. If each letter were instead an arc of its own circle, as in turtle geometry ("turn by αx, then move"), the steps would no longer commute. The position after a word would then depend on the order of its letters. Such curved walks see ordinary repetitions uu, not abelian ones: two factors give congruent pieces of the curve when they are equal as words, not merely anagrams.

Curves do appear in a different way. The imbalance of a factor of length N grows like N0.48, so the walk is a fractal curve in the plane, similar in spirit to Rauzy fractals and dragon curves: the curve of g85n(a) has length 85n, but the region it fills only grows like (√73)n ≈ 8.544n.

7 · What the new geometry suggests

  1. Choose a multiplier, not a morphism. For a cyclically symmetric ternary morphism, the whole action on Parikh vectors is one Eisenstein integer μ, and the walk at block boundaries is μ times the previous walk. Searching for long aa2f words could start from μ: its argument and modulus determine how fast the curve spreads (|μ| against the block length) and which directions repeat. Only then would the images be chosen to realise μ.
  2. Midpoint-free curves. For ternary words, aa2f is the statement that a self-similar lattice curve avoids equal-time midpoints of gap at least 2. Rao and Rosenfeld's avoidance of periods greater than 5 says such curves exist when gaps up to 5 are allowed. Their method can be read as controlling the curve at large scales by the similarity, and checking the small scales by computer.
  3. Near misses as almost-parallel chords. The fragile patterns of g85, such as the 771-pattern, are pairs of chords that are equal and parallel. The junction analysis of the experiments note asks when such chords survive multiplication by −8 + 3i. In the plane, this becomes a question about how rotation by 159.44° moves the short chords at block boundaries.
